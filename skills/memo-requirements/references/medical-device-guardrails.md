# Medical-device guardrails

## Purpose and scope

Requirements are design inputs and records within a controlled development process. This skill helps express them clearly; it does not decide regulatory strategy, product classification, risk acceptability, clinical validity, or conformity.

Use the organization’s approved quality procedures and controlled versions of applicable standards. Do not reproduce or infer requirements from copyrighted standards without an authorized source.

## Design-development framing

Maintain evidence that requirements:

- address intended use and the needs of users and patients;
- are reviewed, approved, and controlled under the organization’s quality system;
- trace to design outputs, verification, and validation evidence;
- include risk-management and change-control links where relevant; and
- handle incomplete, ambiguous, or conflicting inputs through an explicit decision, not hidden wording.

For U.S. finished-device manufacturers, FDA’s Quality Management System Regulation became effective on 2026-02-02 and incorporates ISO 13485:2016 by reference. Use current regulatory advice and the organization’s procedures to determine the actual obligations. FDA distinguishes verifying design output against design input from validating the device against user needs and intended uses.

Sources: [FDA QMSR](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/postmarket-requirements-devices/quality-management-system-regulation-qmsr), [FDA PMA Quality System](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/premarket-approval-pma/pma-quality-system), and [FDA human factors guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/applying-human-factors-and-usability-engineering-medical-devices).

## Risk controls

When a requirement implements a risk control, record the approved risk-management identifiers and avoid unsupported conclusions. State the behavior needed to implement the control, then link the requirement to verification evidence and the risk-management evaluation.

Separate:

- the hazardous situation or harm description;
- the risk-control requirement;
- the residual-risk or benefit-risk decision; and
- the verification evidence that the implemented control behaves as specified.

## Human factors and cybersecurity

Capture the user population, use environment, interface, and critical tasks for requirements affecting interaction. For cybersecurity-related requirements, obtain the organization’s threat model, assets, trust boundaries, security objectives, and required evidence before specifying controls. Avoid generic claims such as “secure,” “encrypted,” or “compliant” without an approved, measurable definition.

## Baseline checklist

Before proposing approval, confirm the baseline has a unique identifier, revision/status, source, rationale, owner, approval route, glossary links, parent/child traceability, risk links as applicable, and verification or validation evidence. Escalate contradictions, missing risk links, unverifiable statements, and claims that may affect labeling, clinical evidence, or regulatory submissions.
